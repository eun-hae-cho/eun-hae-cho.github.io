Research

Different evidence, connected questions.

My proposed study combines experimental evaluation with qualitative description. Coach fidelity, caregiver accuracy, and child requesting remain separate sources of evidence; integration connects them with family experiences and the information available when intervention decisions are made.

Proposed study

One design, three connected questions

Research questions, evidence, and intended inferences
QuestionEvidenceWhat it can establish
RQ1 · Child requestingIndependent functional requests per observable minute in ten-minute routine probesEvaluation of package effects on child requesting through staggered introduction and visual analysis
RQ2 · Caregiver implementationPercentage of applicable instructional steps completed correctly in the same probesSeparate evaluation of package effects on caregiver accuracy
RQ3 · Family experience and decisionsIntake and exit interviews, weekly reflections, field notes, and decision recordsAccounts of learning needs, family priorities, feasibility, burden, and adaptations that contextualize measured outcomes and intervention decisions

The research agenda specifies a concurrent multiple-probe design across four families with autistic children aged three to five. Speech, gestures, signs, and AAC remain eligible communication forms.

Keeping the evidence interpretable

Observe without live coaching

Outcome probes precede coaching when both occur during a visit. Supported practice is recorded separately, so assistance during teaching is not confused with independent caregiver performance.

Evaluate outcomes separately

Coach fidelity describes the support delivered; caregiver accuracy describes procedure use; child requesting describes communication. A functional relation is evaluated separately for each primary outcome. Higher caregiver accuracy does not establish child improvement.

Interpret requests alongside opportunities

Recording opportunities alongside the primary requesting measure would help distinguish more chances to request from a greater proportion of independent responses. This is an interpretive measure, not an expansion of the primary child outcome.

Distinguish learning needs from family fit

Existing caregiver strategies help identify priorities for instruction and rehearsal. Accounts of burden or differing goals may instead prompt reconsideration of timing, materials, or expectations; additional training is not the assumed answer to every difficulty.

Document support and adaptation

Records describe coaching and booster instruction alongside step-specific caregiver performance. Adaptation records distinguish changes to activities or communication forms from changes to core procedures, identifying what changed, when, why, who participated, and what evidence informed the decision.

Examine use beyond teaching

A secondary routine supports generalization assessment, while follow-up approximately two and four weeks after scheduled coaching ends examines maintenance. Family-reported use provides context; direct observations are needed to assess transfer. Any additional support is documented.

Mixed methods integration

Joint displays will align behavioral measures, caregiver accounts, support provided, and dated intervention decisions. Within-family and cross-family analysis will examine agreement, contextual elaboration, and divergence, preserving ambivalent and differing accounts as well as positive experiences.

For example, improved probe performance alongside reports of substantial burden would indicate observed gains with practical constraints on continued use. Positive caregiver evaluations alongside limited requesting change would prompt examination of valued outcomes beyond the measured target.

Collecting feedback is different from showing how it informed a decision. Records will distinguish the information and rationale available during intervention from later explanations. This integration contextualizes outcomes; it does not establish mediation, the separate effects of package components, or a causal effect of an adaptation.
